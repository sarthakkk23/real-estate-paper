The plot city's own price

Plots in Jaipur's new schemes rose far faster than homes.

Jaipur is called a plot city, but the price figures people quote follow homes. I read every plot auction of the Jaipur Development Authority (JDA) to see what land itself did.

126%

Rise in plot prices at JDA's auctions, 2019 to 2025

To one decimal place: +125.8%; anywhere from +88.6% to +174.0%. Only 6 schemes sold plots in both years.

One seller's new plots, not Jaipur's whole land market; each scheme against its own earlier sales. Zone by zone: +96.7% to +105.0%.

38%

Rise in what banks valued Jaipur homes at, the same years

To one decimal place: +38.1%.

The home includes its building, and this index (NHB RESIDEX) averages four quarters, so it moves slowly.

The simple middle price of the same auctions rose only about 47%, because since 2019 JDA has sold far more of its plots in cheaper outer zones. That simple figure hides about half the rise. How the simple figure hides it

I'm Sarthak Oberoi, a data analyst in Jaipur. This is independent research on the city's property market, from public records only: JDA's auction results, RERA Rajasthan's register of projects, the state's registration figures and circle rates, and the National Housing Bank's home-price index. Every number was worked out twice, by two separate programs, and put through hard tests. Each finding says in plain words where it stops. The tests are written out too, set apart, for anyone who wants them. Nothing here is for sale.

Four findings to use on a live file.

Each says when to use it, then the number, where it stops, and how I know. One click opens the full finding.

Use this when you price a large commercial plot, or decide what mix of plot sizes a scheme should sell.

24%

Less per sq m for a commercial (shop) plot of 300-500 sq m (359-598 sq yd) than one of 100-150 sq m (120-179 sq yd), same JDA scheme and year

To one decimal place: −23.8%.

A big commercial (shop) plot fetches less per sq m; a big house plot barely does.

If you value a large commercial plot in a JDA scheme at one rate per sq m, you overstate it by about a fifth to a quarter. For a residential layout, one rate per sq m across sizes is a fair start.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. The gap between commercial and house plots could partly be chance: it did not pass my strictest test. JDA sets its starting price per sq m, which may pull bids towards one rate. The very largest house plots rest on few sales.

How I know
  • What I read. About 2,245 commercial-plot auctions and 4,070 house-plot auctions at JDA, each compared only with plots in the same scheme and the same year.
  • How I compared. Each plot's rate per sq m against a plot of 100-150 sq m in the same scheme and year, allowing for corners, the month and whether it was an online auction.
  • How sure. For the big commercial plots, it could be as small as −16.8% or as large as −30.2%. Either way it is a clear discount.

Use this when you value a plot from a comparable sale, or a client is quoted a rate that looks far from the scheme's usual.

±14%

How close a comparable from the same JDA scheme, use and year gets you: 8 in 10 plots sold within about this much of the scheme's usual rate

To one decimal place: ±14.2%.

A comparable from the same scheme and year is good to about 14% either way.

Most of the risk in a valuation is in bringing a rate to another year, not in the plot itself. Carried to a year the sums had not seen, the miss was far wider: from about 28% below the guess to about 39% above it, for 8 plots in 10.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions, not the open market. One test year only, and a year of fast rises. The band looked wider in 2020-24, but that could be chance, so I claim no trend.

How I know
  • What I read. 371 groups of plots that share a JDA scheme, a use and a year, each with enough auctions to measure a spread: about 6,000 auctions in all (5,973).
  • How I measured the band. In each group, how far winning rates spread once size and corners are allowed for. I report the typical group.
  • The test on a hidden year. I hid all 338 plot auctions of 2026, fitted my sums on 2003-2025, made them guess 2026, then compared with what really sold.

Use this when you write the note on what to build on a Jaipur site: plotted colony or apartments.

6 in 10

New RERA projects in Jaipur district that were plotted schemes in 2025, up from about 3 in 10 in 2018

To one decimal place: 57.4% in 2025 (261 of 455) and 32.4% in 2018 (36 of 111).

Plotted schemes now lead Jaipur's new RERA registrations, on a long trend.

The move from apartments to plots in Jaipur did not start with one rule change at the end of 2024. You can put it in a highest-and-best-use note as a long shift in what gets registered, not as a one-year spike.

Where it stops RERA counts projects or phases, not homes or area. A project's year is the year in its registration number. 67% of the rise in the plotted share came before the state's circular of 30 December 2024, which may still have added to 2025.

How I know
  • What I read. The counts that RERA Rajasthan's public search shows for each project type and year, read on 13 and 14 November 2026.
  • Read twice. All 20 Jaipur counts by type and year came out the same on both days.
  • How I tested "no break". I fitted the trend from 2018 to 2024 only, then asked what it expected for 2025. It expected about 53%; 2025 came in at about 57%, close enough to be chance.

Use this when a feasibility report or a valuation has to say which price it quotes.

−3.4% to +64.6%

How much Jaipur's published price figures moved, each over its own twelve months, all within July 2022 to December 2024: from builders' quoted prices to new launch prices

Jaipur's published prices disagree, so name the one you quote.

Each figure answers a different question: what a new launch asks, what a bank will lend against, what a deed records, what a plot fetched. Pick the one that matches your question and cite it by its publisher.

Where it stops Each publisher measures over its own twelve months, so the periods do not match exactly. Housing.com's figure is quoted from a news report, not checked at its source. No figure is ranked as the right one.

How I know
  • What I read. Each figure from its own publisher (PropEquity, NHB, RBI, the listing websites), or worked out here from JDA's results.
  • Checked. I re-read RBI's −0.3% in its release of 31 May 2024. The comparison table was recomputed by separate code and every row agreed.
  • Kept out. A circle-rate index I tried is held back (it rests on too few offices) and is not shown anywhere.
