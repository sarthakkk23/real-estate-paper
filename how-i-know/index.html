How I know, in plain words.

Every number on this site comes from a public record, and every one was worked out twice by programs written apart. Below, each method is a plain question with a plain answer. Tap "See it work" for a small picture made from the site's own figures. The technical names are at the end, for analysts.

Which records did I read, and when?

Each public record I used, and the date I read it. Where I could not read a record on a date, I say so and nothing more.

  • JDA's auction results. The Jaipur Development Authority (JDA) is the city's planning body. It sells plots in its own schemes by auction and prints every result on one public page: date, zone, scheme, use, size and the winning rate. It is one seller's plots, so every figure from it says "at JDA's auctions". Read once, on 13 November 2026, covering November 2003 to 10 November 2026.
  • RERA Rajasthan's project search. RERA is the state's real estate regulator. A project above a set size must register with it before it sells. I read how many projects the public search shows under each filter, one page view at a time. Its data service does not answer programs, so I stopped there: I have counts of projects, not the list, and a project is not a home. Read on 13 November and again on 14 November 2026.
  • Circle rates (the state's e-Panjiyan portal). A circle rate is the lowest value per sq m the state sets for each colony. Stamp duty is charged on it or on the price, whichever is higher. It is a floor for tax, not a price. The portal's terms bar copying its rates, so I show only ratios, steps and shares by registration office, never a rupee rate. Current rates and dated histories read from 13 November to 15 November 2026. On two nights I could not read it; each time I stopped at once and have not gone back, so 9 offices have no dates yet.
  • The state's registration dashboard. Monthly counts of registered documents and sale deeds, by district. A sub-registrar office is the government office where a sale deed is registered and stamp duty paid. 67 months for 41 districts, read on 13 and 14 November 2026.
  • NHB RESIDEX. The National Housing Bank's index of home values in Jaipur, built from the valuations banks make when they lend on a home. Read on 13 November 2026. I could not read it again on 15 November, so it rests on one reading.
  • The Finance Department's notifications. The orders that set stamp duty, the surcharges and the registration fee. Read in November 2026, on the department's website or in signed copies.

I also quote published reports by name and date: Liases Foras's reports for CREDAI (to September 2023), the Reserve Bank of India's house price index releases, and JDA's zone order of 10 October 2025.

How did I clean the auction list?

JDA's page had 8,479 rows. 33 were exact copies of another row, so I removed them, leaving 8,446. 76 rows sold a built space (a shop, a flat, a floor), not land, so I set them aside. I also left out plain typing errors, and a few rates far from the same scheme's other sales that I could not check.

What is left is 7,316 sales of land of at least 25 sq m. Every land figure on this site uses them. I checked that the area is in sq m and the rate in rupees per sq m against the page's own totals.

How do I compare land prices fairly?

The simplest figure is the middle price: half the plots sold above it, half below. But it follows where JDA happened to sell. If one year's auctions are mostly in cheaper outer schemes, the middle price falls even when every scheme got dearer.

So I compare each scheme only with its own earlier sales, allowing for size, use and corner, and then put the schemes together. From 2019 to 2025 the simple middle price rose +47.4%. Compared scheme by scheme, land rose +125.8%. I tried 8 fair ways of comparing; they give +96.7% to +220.4%. I lead with the scheme-by-scheme figure, which keeps the plots most alike, and give the zone-by-zone figure, +96.7%, beside it wherever it applies.

How do I know the sums work on a year they have not seen?

A method can fit the past well and still guess badly. So I hid 2026 from the sums, made them guess the price of each of 2026's 338 land auctions, then compared each guess with what the plot really fetched.

Half the guesses were within 14.1% of the real price. A simple rule of thumb, the middle price of the same scheme and use over the 12 months before, was within 18.4% on the 276 plots it could price. My sums did better. When a method does worse than the rule of thumb, I say so and hold it back: the plot rate estimator did, and it waits on What I'm not claiming yet.

How many tests did I run, and how many passed?

When you run a hundred tests, a few will look real by luck alone. So I held every result to a stricter bar, one that gets harder to pass the more tests you run.

I ran 110 tests. 26 passed the strict bar, about 2 in 10. Where a page mentions a result that did not pass, it says beside it that it could be chance.

How do I know the arithmetic is right?

Every number was worked out twice, by two programs written apart from each other, from the same records. The site is built only when the two agree on every figure it prints.

The statistics: 272 of 272 agree. JDA's land figures: 38 of 38. RERA's headline counts: 36 of 36. Before that, a separate review recomputed 1,074 numbers in my first drafts: 1,015 matched, 11 did not (all small differences) and 48 could not be checked because the outside figures they used were not in its files. I also took 113 rules and dates back to their original source: 51 were confirmed and 12 corrected. The rest I could not find or open at source. Those are either left out, or shown with what they rest on instead, such as an audit report, or "no order found".

What the checks changed

  • Words dropped. "Three times as fast as homes", a reading of how many bids JDA's auctions draw, "the same schemes fetched +30.4% more after mid-2022", "the outer ring led", an interest-rate explanation, and "the five lowest-priced zones" (they are the outer zones 10 to 14, not the five cheapest).
  • Numbers corrected. The value of all land JDA has sold since 2003 is ₹9,800.2 crore, worked from the sales themselves, not from rounded yearly totals. The extra paid for commercial land now uses only schemes that sold both uses (in 2020-22, 1,082 auctions in 14 schemes rather than 1,204 in 15). Circle rates were cut twice before 2022, not three times. The 2026 circle-rate revision is dated 30 July or 31 July in the portal, not 3 August. RERA projects marked "Completed" for 2024 are 23 to 34 months past registration.
  • A claim made more careful. "No policy date shows a price break" was wrong as written. What holds is that no policy date shows a shift that points the same way whether or not the steady rise is allowed for.
  • A second look at JDA's page. JDA prints a zone only from 4 September 2019. Zones before then are my own assignment, so the zone-by-zone figure is given as the lowest of its readings.

The ground rules

  • Totals only. Nothing per plot, per deed, per project or per colony.
  • JDA's auctions are not the market. They are one government seller's plots in its own schemes, so every figure from them says "at JDA's auctions".
  • Counts mean what they count. RERA counts projects, not homes. A circle rate is a floor for stamp duty, not a price.
  • No rupee rate from the circle-rate portal. Its terms bar copying its rates; I show ratios, steps and shares by office.
  • Two numbers, never a ratio. Land and homes are shown side by side, never as "so many times faster".
  • No forecast. No price prediction and no "which area next".
  • Every limit beside its number. If a figure has a catch, the catch sits next to it.
  • Praise, never blame. No authority, firm, area or community is criticised. Where I could not read a record on a date, I say only that.

Short notes on the records

Things a careful reader should know before quoting any of these records.

  • Which count? Homes sold in Jaipur in 2024 were reported as 10,695 (PropEquity, February 2025), 10,271 (the same firm's restated figure, February 2026) and "over 8,450" (CRE Matrix, December 2025). Sale deeds registered in Jaipur district were 1,82,067 in 2024 and 1,87,961 in 2025, but a sale deed is any registered transfer: plots, farm land, resales. So reported new-home sales (9,758 in 2025) are a small slice of the deeds, not a rival count. Always quote the release date with the number.
  • Labels on the register RERA's status words are what a project files, not what an inspector found. "Completed" was shown for 57.3% of Jaipur projects registered in 2024, only 23 to 34 months after registration. "Lapsed" means the registered period ran out, not that the project failed. About 3% are marked revoked or suspended: 66 of the 2,416 Jaipur projects on the register. Counts are projects, not homes.
  • The circle-rate table 27 registration offices list urban rates, in 4,512 colony rows. About 7 in 10 of the difference between colonies' residential rates lies between offices, not within one: the office matters more than the colony. Some offices put many colonies on one rate. Revision dates come from the portal's own histories; I could not open the orders. In words only: JDA's residential auctions clear well above the residential circle rate, and its commercial auctions much closer to it.
  • JDA's zones JDA prints a zone only from 4 September 2019. Its region took in more villages in October 2025 and its zone order of 10 October 2025 sets out more zones than its results page prints (zones 1 to 14). No scheme has changed zone. At each refresh I check for a new zone.
  • Bank valuations move slowly by design NHB RESIDEX averages each quarter with the three before it (the Economic Survey of 2021-22 describes this). So it turns gently, and a sharp change in prices shows up in it late and softened.

For analysts

For analysts: the technical name of each method, the counts, and how to re-run a figure. Each line starts with the plain question it answers.

  • Which records? Sources and reads. JDA post-auction results, one public page, read once as a single page. RERA Rajasthan public search: counts under each filter, 168 page views over both readings; 98 of 100 counts read twice were identical, and 60 more were read once. Circle rates: 4,512 colony rows in 27 offices; dated histories for a sample of colonies in 7 offices and one colony in each of 11 more (277 history pop-ups, 3,690 rate lines); 9 offices undated. Registration dashboard: 67 months by 41 districts; sale deeds comparable only from April 2024. NHB RESIDEX HPI@Assessment, Jaipur, calendar-year means of the quarters.
  • How was the list cleaned? Deduplication and the index sample. 33 exact duplicate rows of 8,479 dropped; 76 built-unit rows excluded; keying errors and unverifiable outliers against the same scheme's sales excluded. Index sample: land auctions of at least 25 sq m, 7,316 rows, November 2003 to 10 November 2026. Zones as printed by JDA from 4 September 2019; earlier zones assigned by me from the scheme.
  • How are land prices compared fairly? Repeat-scheme hedonic index. Log rate on scheme fixed effects and year effects, with use, size band and corner; 2019 to 2025 +125.8%. Scheme-clustered bootstrap (200 draws of whole schemes) +88.6% to +174.0%; leave-one-scheme-out +112.9% to +138.0%; leave-one-zone-out +112.5% to +138.2%. Alternatives: hedonic within zones +96.7% (up to +105.0% without my 2019 zone assignments), stratified median +118.4%, chained matched-scheme with count, minimum-count, value and equal weights (+220.4%, +139.3%, +96.8%, +106.7%), chained median ratios +119.4%; plain median +47.4%.
  • Do the sums work on an unseen year? Out-of-sample holdout of the scheme model. The scheme model: log rate on scheme and year effects, use, size band by use, corner by use, month and auction format; errors clustered by scheme; 7,316 index auctions in 523 schemes. Fitted to 2003-2025 with the 2023-25 drift, tested on 338 auctions of 2026: median absolute percentage error 14.1%; errors from −28.0% (10th percentile) to +38.6% (90th); the model's own 80% interval (±25.5%) covered 66.6%. Naive benchmark, the trailing 12-month median of the same scheme and use: 18.4% on 276 auctions.
  • Why is the estimator held? Holdout of model PZ against the benchmark. PZ is the scheme model with zone effects in place of scheme effects and size bands. On 2026: 31.2% against the benchmark's 18.4%, on the same 276 auctions. On 2025 (fitted to 2003-2024): 22.6% against 27.7%.
  • How many tests passed? Multiple-testing correction. 110 p-values across the statistical analysis. 26 remain significant at the five per cent level after Holm's family-wise correction across all of them, and 37 after Benjamini-Hochberg's false-discovery-rate correction. Each finding page names the tests behind it.
  • Is the arithmetic right? Independent recomputation. Statistics recomputed by separately written code: 272 of 272 agree. JDA headline numbers re-derived by separate code: 38 of 38. RERA headline numbers: 36 of 36. One script builds the figures file and a second, written separately, re-fits the indexes from the auction rows and re-reads the raw RERA counts; the site builds only if every printed figure matches.
  • How do I re-run a figure? Reproduction. Every page ends with "Every number on this page", saying what each figure counts. The aggregated tables behind the charts are on the Data page. For the land indexes, start from JDA's public results page and follow the cleaning and the model above; the figures should land within rounding.
