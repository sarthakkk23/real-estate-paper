At JDA's auctions, a commercial plot of 300 to 500 sq m, in the same scheme and year, would typically go for about ₹33,244 per sq m.

How sure

Eight in ten plots like this sold within about 14.2% either side of the typical rate in their scheme and year. So it could be as low as ₹28,539 or as high as ₹37,949 per sq m. This range is for the plot only. The size step adds its own uncertainty, shown below, so treat the range as the least it could be.

How sure the size step is. In the same scheme and year, commercial plots of 300 to 500 sq m sold for between 16.8% and 30.2% less per sq m than plots of 100 to 150 sq m.

Limit

Same scheme and same year only: carried to another year or another scheme, a rate is far less sure. The corner premium for residential plots does not pass the stricter check for testing many things at once; the commercial one does. This describes JDA's auctions only, one seller's plots, not Jaipur's whole land market.

Source

JDA's post-auction results, read 13 November 2026: 4,070 residential and 2,245 commercial auctions in the size steps; the spread from 5,973 auctions in 371 groups of one scheme, one use and one year.

For analysts

How this was tested (for analysts)
  • Each size step compares plots only with other plots in the same scheme and the same year, so a dear scheme or a boom year does not pass for a size effect (regression of the log rate on size bands with scheme and year effects, errors clustered by scheme).
  • How far a comparable can be off is measured, not assumed: in each group of plots from one scheme, one use and one year, I measured how far winning rates sit from the group's typical rate after size and corner (a band holding eight in ten plots, from the median within-group standard deviation of the log rate).
  • The corner premiums were put through a stricter test that allows for many tests run at once (Holm correction).
  • A second, separate script recomputes every size step and both corner premiums and must agree (independent cross-check).
  • The page's own sums are run on worked cases and must match a second, separately written version to the rupee.
