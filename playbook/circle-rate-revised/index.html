Case 3 of 6 · Due diligence

“The circle rate was revised. What changes in my cost line?”

The short answer

In the SANGANER-II office the duty base rose by about 62% across the two 2026 revisions. Redo the stamp-duty line office by office, never with one city percentage.

A feasibility written in March 2026 for land in the SANGANER-II registration office used the circle rate then in force as the base for stamp duty. Since then the rates have been revised twice: once in April and again in July. What changes in the cost line?

The figure to keep in mind

Circle rates are the minimum values for stamp duty, not prices. Where the circle value of the land is higher than the price paid, duty is charged on the circle value. That is when a revision moves your cost line.

The worked example

The duty base before the revisions is an example: ₹50 lakh, the round figure the cost calculator starts from. The two steps are what I read from the circle-rate portal's own history of each rate.

If you multiply the two rounded steps shown here you get a tenth of a point less than +62.0%: the difference is rounding. The cost of buying is 8.98% of the base both times; only the base has moved.

The July 2026 revision differed by office

The middle step in residential circle rates, July 2026, in the offices I sampled. SANGANER-II, the office in this case, is in the darker colour. SANGANER-I rests on fewer colony rates (16) than the others.

The circle-rate portal's own history of each rate, read between 13 and 15 November 2026. Steps only; no rupee level is shown.

Every step of the sum 5 steps

  1. 01

    The April 2026 round

    In April 2026 almost every rate rose by the same step, +10.0%: exactly that in 410 of the 421 colony rates I read (a colony rate is one colony's rate for one use).

  2. 02

    The July 2026 revision, in this office

    In SANGANER-II the residential rates I sampled rose by +47.2% in the middle case: half of the 36 colony rates rose more, half less. Each step is measured from the rate in force just before July, so it comes on top of April's.

    For analyststhe middle half of the steps ran from +46.5% to +47.6%.

  3. 03

    Put the two together

    The steps multiply, they do not add: grow the old rate by +10.0%, then grow that by +47.2%. Together that is +62.0%.

  4. 04

    What it does to the duty base and the cost of buying

    If the circle value is the duty base, the base rises by +62.0%. The example base of ₹50 lakh becomes ₹80.99 lakh. For a man buying alone, stamp duty, surcharges and the registration fee go from ₹4.49 lakh to ₹7.27 lakh.

  5. 05

    Check the office, not the city

    The revision was not one city figure. The walled city's office, JAIPUR-VI, rose +18.9% in the middle case; SANGANER-II rose +47.2%. The office comparator puts the offices side by side.

What it does and does not tell you

What it tells you

  • How far the duty base moved in one office across the two 2026 revisions, and what that does to the cost of buying.
  • That the July step differed a great deal from office to office.

What it does not tell you

  • What land in the office is worth. Circle rates are minimum values for stamp duty, not prices.
  • Your colony's own step. These are colonies I sampled, not every colony, and they are not weighted by how many deeds each sees.
  • Every office's date: 9 of the 27 offices that list urban rates were not dated before the portal stopped answering.
  • Any rupee rate. The portal's terms bar reproducing its rates, so only steps and ratios appear here.

What to do this week

  1. Read the date the portal prints for the office. Most offices I could date (16 of 18) show their current rate from the last days of July 2026; check yours.
  2. Check whether the colony's row is new in July 2026. In one office I sampled, 27 of the 34 colonies I read had rows that were new in July 2026, so there is no older rate to compare with.
  3. Redo the duty line office by office, never with one city percentage. Use the step for the office the land sits in.

The tests behind this

The tests behind this, for analysts

One plain sentence per test, the technical name in brackets.

  • A step is a colony's new rate divided by the rate in force just before, both read from the portal's own history of that rate (no rupee level is kept on the site).
  • None of the 421 July 2026 steps I measured was a fall (0).
  • The office figure is the middle step of the sampled colony rates, not an average weighted by deeds.

The findings behind this case

  • The July 2026 circle-rate revision moved each office by a very different amount.
  • Which office you are in decides most of a circle rate.
