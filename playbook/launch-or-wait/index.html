Case 6 of 6 · Timing

“Should my client buy at a scheme's launch, or wait?”

The short answer

Inside a scheme, timing made no difference at JDA's auctions. A scheme still selling after a year is usually a cheaper scheme. Choose the scheme, not the moment.

A plot investor asks whether JDA's first auctions in a new scheme are a bargain, or whether he should wait for later rounds in the same scheme.

The figure to keep in mind

Inside the same JDA scheme, plots sold in its first year went for about the same as plots sold in its second year (−0.8%): no launch discount, and no launch premium.

The worked example

This case is a reading, not a sum: four steps, each a checked figure.

The scheme matters; the moment inside it does not

Inside one scheme: plots sold in its first year against plots sold in its second. Across schemes: schemes in their first year against schemes in their second, in the same zone and year.

JDA's post-auction results, read to 10 November 2026; my sums. At JDA's auctions only.

The reading, step by step 4 steps

  1. 01

    Inside a scheme: first year against second year

    Comparing each plot only with plots in its own scheme, plots sold in the scheme's first year went for −0.8% against its second year: about the same. The true figure could be as low as −7.1% or as high as +5.9%: no sign of a discount either way.

    For analysts6,596 auctions in 458 schemes; scheme effects plus zone-by-year effects and plot attributes, errors clustered by scheme. The method assumes prices stop changing with a scheme's age after its third year, and trusts ages only for schemes first seen from 2007; other age limits were not run.

  2. 02

    Across schemes: a scheme still selling in its second year is usually a cheaper scheme

    Comparing different schemes in the same zone and year, plots in schemes in their first year went for +23.3% more than plots in schemes in their second year. That is about which schemes are still selling, not about the plots getting cheaper with time. It is a pattern across schemes, never a statement about a named one.

    For analyststhe contrast runs from +10.7% to +37.4% (95% range) and passed my strictest test; the second-year term on its own (−20.7%) did not, so I quote the contrast, not that term.

  3. 03

    No month is reliably cheaper

    Plots sold in February and March went for +4.2% against the other months once the steady rise through each year is allowed for, but −2.0% without it. The answer flips with the way you count, which is the finding: there is no reliable month to bid.

    For analyststhe number of plots sold shows no month pattern either (joint p 0.085).

  4. 04

    A plot that comes back to auction is no bargain

    Plots that came back to auction resold in step with their scheme's prices: the typical one sold +0.7% away from where the scheme's prices said it should. JDA's page does not say why a plot returns, so never read it as a default or an overbid.

    For analystsslope 1.10 against the same-scheme index (109 pairs; one would mean exactly in line).

What it does and does not tell you

What it tells you

  • That rushing a launch, or waiting for later rounds in the same scheme, made no measurable difference at JDA's auctions.
  • That a scheme still auctioning after a year is, as a pattern, a cheaper scheme: something to ask about.

What it does not tell you

  • That the second-year scheme's plots are cheaper because of their age. It is a selection of schemes, not ageing.
  • Anything about a named scheme.
  • Why a plot came back to auction.
  • What happens outside JDA's auctions.

Where to go from here

No calculator does this one: it is a reading of checked figures, not a sum on your numbers. The findings behind it are linked below.

What to do this week

  1. Choose the scheme, not the moment. Compare schemes on location, use and what they have sold for, not on how new they are.
  2. Treat a scheme still auctioning after a year as a question for due diligence. Ask why its plots are still on offer.
  3. Do not expect a discount on a re-auctioned plot. Value it like any other plot in its scheme.

Sources

  • JDA, post-auction results page: every land auction listed, Nov 2003 to 10 November 2026, read to 10 November 2026.

The tests behind this

The tests behind this, for analysts

One plain sentence per test, the technical name in brackets.

  • Launch: each plot compared only with plots in its own scheme, allowing for zone, year, size, use and corner (a regression with scheme and zone-by-year effects, errors clustered by scheme), recomputed by separate code.
  • Second-year schemes: the gap between first-year and second-year schemes in the same zone and year passed Holm's correction across all my tests.
  • Months: month effects with the drift inside each year taken out, and monthly counts of plots sold (a quasi-Poisson model).
  • Re-auctioned plots: each returned plot's change set against its scheme's index over the same dates, with uncertainty from redrawing whole schemes 1,000 times (a scheme bootstrap).

The findings behind this case

  • There is no launch discount inside a JDA scheme.
  • A scheme still auctioning in its second year is usually a cheaper scheme.
  • There is no reliable month to bid at JDA.
  • A re-auctioned plot resells in line with its scheme.
