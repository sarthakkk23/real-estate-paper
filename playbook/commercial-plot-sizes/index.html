Case 2 of 6 · Pricing

“What plot sizes should a commercial scheme sell?”

The short answer

At JDA's auctions, small commercial (shop) plots fetched about 1.5 times the rate per sq m of large ones in the same scheme and year. For commercial land, size matters; for house plots it hardly does.

A developer is laying out commercial plots inside a planned scheme. He asks whether to cut the land into small plots or large ones, and what each would fetch per sq m.

The figure to keep in mind

In the same JDA scheme and year, a 300-500 sq m commercial plot fetched −23.8% per sq m against a 100-150 sq m one. A 25-100 sq m commercial plot fetched +14.1%.

The worked example

There is no example input here: every number comes from JDA's own auctions. The base is a commercial plot of 100-150 sq m (120-179 sq yd) sold in the same scheme and year.

The same land sold as small plots fetches, per sq m, this many times what it fetches as large plots1.50 times

Take the base rate as one whole. Small plots fetch one whole plus 14%; large plots fetch one whole less 24%. Divide the first by the second and you get 1.50.

Commercial plots: the bigger the plot, the lower the rate per sq m

JDA's post-auction results, read to 10 November 2026; my sums.

Every step of the sum 4 steps

  1. 01

    Commercial against residential, in the same scheme

    At JDA's auctions, a commercial plot of about 150 sq m fetched +51.3% more per sq m than a residential plot of the same size in the same scheme.

    For analystsit could be as low as +36.5% or as high as +67.8% (a 95% range); it passed my strictest test.

  2. 02

    How the commercial rate changes with size

    Against a 100-150 sq m commercial plot in the same scheme and year: 25-100 sq m fetched +14.1% per sq m; 200-300 sq m, −18.6%; 300-500 sq m, −23.8%; 1,000 sq m and over, −30.1%. The bigger the plot, the lower the rate per sq m.

    For analyststhe large-plot step could be as low as −30.2% or as high as −16.8%; the small-plot step from +6.7% to +22.1% (95% ranges).

  3. 03

    Small plots against large plots, for the same land

    Sold as 25-100 sq m plots rather than 300-500 sq m plots, the same land fetched 1.50 times as much per sq m.

  4. 04

    Residential, for contrast

    For house plots the rule does not hold. From 25-100 sq m to 300-500 sq m, residential plots sat between −5.4% and −0.2% of the same base: size barely moves the rate.

What it does and does not tell you

What it tells you

  • That in JDA's schemes, commercial land cut into small plots fetched a clearly higher rate per sq m than the same land in large plots.
  • That one rate per sq m applied to a large commercial parcel overstates it, by about a fifth to a quarter.

What it does not tell you

  • How fast each size sells. That is not measured here.
  • What the extra road and frontage cost: smaller plots need more of both, which eats into the gain.
  • Anything outside JDA's schemes: these are convenience plots inside planned schemes, not high-street land.
  • Whether the size effect is truly stronger for commercial than for residential plots. The gap between the two could be chance: it did not pass my strictest test.
  • Whether bids are anchored: JDA sets its auctions per sq m, which may pull bids towards its own figure.

What to do this week

  1. Test a smaller-plot option in the commercial mix. Price it with the size steps above, then subtract the extra road and frontage.
  2. Do not apply residential's flat size rule to commercial land. What holds for house plots does not hold for shops and offices.
  3. For a large commercial parcel, build a size discount of about a fifth to a quarter into the bid. One rate per sq m from small plots will overstate it.

Sources

  • JDA, post-auction results page: every land auction listed, Nov 2003 to 10 November 2026, read to 10 November 2026.

The tests behind this

The tests behind this, for analysts

One plain sentence per test, the technical name in brackets.

  • Each plot is compared only with plots sold in the same scheme and year, allowing for use, size, corner, month and the kind of auction (a regression with scheme and year effects, errors clustered by scheme; 2,245 commercial and 4,070 residential auctions).
  • Five of the six commercial size steps passed my strictest test, which allows for the number of tests I ran (Holm's correction).
  • How fast the rate falls with size: for commercial plots −0.145, from −0.209 to −0.081 (an elasticity: change in log rate per change in log area).
  • The commercial-minus-residential gap in that fall, −0.098 (p 0.0084), did not survive Holm's correction; with zone-by-year effects the residential figure is −0.024, not different from zero.
  • The figures were recomputed by separately written code before they went on the site.

The findings behind this case

  • A big commercial (shop) plot fetches less per sq m; a big house plot barely does.
  • A commercial (shop) plot fetches about half as much again as a house plot in the same scheme.
