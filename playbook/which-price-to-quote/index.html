Case 4 of 6 · Valuing

“Which price do I quote in a feasibility report?”

The short answer

The one that answers your question, named and dated. In readings that fall within July 2022 to December 2024, each over its own period, Jaipur's published price measures ran from −3.4% to +64.6%, so the choice changes the report.

A feasibility for a mid-segment apartment project needs a price line and a growth line. There are several published "prices" for Jaipur homes, and each answers a different question.

The figure to keep in mind

In readings that fall within July 2022 to December 2024, Jaipur's published price measures ran from −3.4% (builders' quotes, July-September 2023 against a year earlier) to +64.6% (launch prices of new projects, 2023 to January-September 2024). None of them is wrong: they measure different things, each over its own period.

Every step of the sum 3 steps

  1. 01

    Name the question first

    A new flat's price is a launch price: ₹6,979 per sq ft (Jan-Sep 2024, the latest I found). What sellers ask today is an asking price: ₹4,950 to ₹6,432 per sq ft on two portals (Sep and Oct 2026). What existing homes are worth is a bank valuation: up +12.5% from Jun 2025 to Jun 2026.

  2. 02

    Pick the growth figure that matches the question, and say which it is

    Each of these covers its own stretch of time, within July 2022 to December 2024: builders' quotes −3.4% (July-September 2023 against a year earlier), registered prices −0.3% (January-March 2024 against a year earlier), bank valuations +7.6% (the 2023 average to the 2024 average), land at JDA's auctions +19.9% (2023 to 2024), launch prices +64.6% (2023 to January-September 2024). A report that quotes one of these should name it and its period.

  3. 03

    Add the buyer's cost on top of the price

    Above ₹10 lakh, a man buying alone pays 8.98% of the value in stamp duty, surcharges and the registration fee. Multiply the price by 1.0898 to get what the buyer pays in all.

What it does and does not tell you

What it tells you

  • Which published price answers which question, with its period.
  • How much the growth line changes depending on which measure you pick.

What it does not tell you

  • Which measure is "right". None is ranked: each answers a different question.
  • One common period for all of them: each measure covers its own stretch of time, named beside it.
  • A launch price that compares the same kind of flat each time: a launch average moves with the mix of what launched.
  • A full series for builders' quotes: only the published year-on-year figure is available.

What to do this week

  1. Label every price in the report: launch, asking, valuation, registered, or circle rate.
  2. Give each price its release date and its period. A launch price from 2024 is not today's price.
  3. Never apply a launch-price growth rate to an existing home's value. Use bank valuations or registered prices for existing homes.

Sources

  • Launch prices and builders' quotes: PropEquity, as published (launch price, Jan-Sep 2024).
  • Asking prices: Square Yards (Sep 2026) and Housing.com (Oct 2026).
  • Bank valuations: NHB RESIDEX, HPI@Assessment Prices, Jaipur.
  • Registered prices: RBI's house price index for Jaipur, release of 31 May 2024.
  • Cost of buying: Rajasthan Finance Department notifications F.4(4)FD/Tax/2020-135, F.4(2)FD/Tax/2026-132, F.2(9)FD/Tax/2020-176 and F.4(2)FD/Tax/2024-68, read in November 2026.

The tests behind this

The tests behind this, for analysts

One plain sentence per test, the technical name in brackets.

  • Each measure was read from its publisher or computed here, and the comparison was recomputed by separately written code.
  • No statistical test ranks the measures: the point is what each one measures, not which is right.

The findings behind this case

  • Jaipur's published prices disagree, so name the one you quote.
