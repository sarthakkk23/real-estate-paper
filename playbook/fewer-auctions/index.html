Case 5 of 6 · Timing

“JDA's auctions fell sharply. Is land softening in my corridor?”

The short answer

Not from this evidence. JDA sold fewer plots, but across the zones the ones where it sold fewer did not see lower prices, and nothing in its data says which way next year goes.

A land owner near a JDA scheme in zone 12 reads that JDA sold far fewer plots in 2025. He asks whether land around him is getting softer, and whether he should sell now.

The figure to keep in mind

In 6 of 7 JDA zones, JDA sold fewer plots in 2025 than in 2024, and prices at its auctions still rose in 5 of them. Across the city JDA sold 555 plots in 2025 against 1,245 in 2024 (−55.4%), while prices, comparing each scheme only with its own earlier sales, rose +23.4%.

The worked example

This case is a reading, not a sum: five steps, each a checked figure.

The reading, step by step 5 steps

  1. 01

    JDA sold fewer plots

    JDA's results page lists 555 land sales in 2025 against 1,245 in 2024: −55.4%. Leave out the small shop sites and the fall is −58.8%. These are sales, not plots put up for auction, and the value agreed, not cash received.

  2. 02

    Prices at JDA's auctions did not fall

    Comparing each scheme only with its own earlier sales, prices at JDA's auctions rose +23.4% from 2024 to 2025.

  3. 03

    Zone 12 in 2025

    In zone 12, the number of plots JDA sold changed by −64% against 2024, and its prices, compared scheme by scheme, moved +27.5%.

  4. 04

    Across the zones, fewer sales did not mean lower prices

    In 6 of 7 zones JDA sold fewer plots in 2025, and prices still rose in 5 of them. Over every zone and year side by side (41 of them), the same holds: where JDA sold fewer plots, prices did not move lower. The link is about as close to nothing as it gets, and could easily be chance.

    For analystsfor each one per cent change in the number of auctions, price moved about 0.031 per cent (an elasticity), from −0.026 to 0.089 (95% range), permutation p 0.39; 1 zone-year (zone 7, 2023) set aside, because its year change cannot be separated from the corner term.

  5. 05

    Nothing in the data says which way next year goes

    I tried every pattern I could find in JDA's own data: this year's change, how many plots sold, the change in that, how many plots sold at the minimum step, how many came back to auction. Together, none guessed next year's zone price better than simply assuming every zone moves with the year's average.

    For analystsleave-one-year-out R² −0.48 on 28 zone-years (below zero means worse than the year average).

What it does and does not tell you

What it tells you

  • That JDA put far fewer plots through its auctions in 2025.
  • That, across the zones, the ones where JDA sold fewer plots did not see lower prices at its auctions.

What it does not tell you

  • That land values in the corridor fell. Fewer sales by one seller is not a fall in price.
  • Which way next year goes. Nothing in the data I tested predicts it.
  • How many plots JDA offered: these are sales, not plots put up for auction.
  • That supply caused anything. It is a small panel of zones and years.
  • Anything about Jaipur's plot market as a whole: these are JDA's sales only.

Where to go from here

No calculator does this one: it is a reading of checked figures, not a sum on your numbers. The findings behind it are linked below.

What to do this week

  1. Ask JDA for its offer lists: the plots put up for auction, not only the plots sold.
  2. Do not use JDA's auction numbers as a measure of demand. A fall in JDA's sales is about what JDA offered.
  3. For demand, look at registrations. Jaipur's sale deeds were flat, with no boom or bust: +2.0% a year, from April 2024 to October 2026 (it could be anywhere from −1.5% to +5.7%). They count every sale deed, not new homes, over a short window.

Sources

  • JDA, post-auction results page: every land auction listed, Nov 2003 to 10 November 2026, read to 10 November 2026.
  • Registrations: the Rajasthan registration department's public dashboard, monthly sale deeds by district.

The tests behind this

The tests behind this, for analysts

One plain sentence per test, the technical name in brackets.

  • Volume and price: each zone's change in price, comparing each scheme only with its own earlier sales, set against its change in auctions listed, allowing for the year (a regression clustered by zone, checked by shuffling the volumes within each year; 41 zone-years).
  • Next year: five indicators, each year left out in turn and guessed from the others (leave-one-year-out prediction), on 28 zone-years; every single indicator also failed a shuffle test.
  • Registrations: Jaipur's monthly sale deeds with the season taken out, tested for a trend and for a break.

The findings behind this case

  • JDA sold less than half as many plots in 2025 as in 2024, while its prices rose.
  • Fewer auctions in a zone did not mean weaker prices there.
  • Nothing in JDA's data tells you next year's price in a zone.
  • Registrations show no Jaipur boom or bust, April 2024 to October 2026.
