Finding · Which plot

A corner shows up in the top bids, not the floor.

Use this when you price a corner plot, or a bidder asks how much extra a corner is worth at a JDA auction.

+14.2%

Extra for a corner plot among the highest bids at JDA's auctions, each plot compared with its own scheme (a corner premium, not the band round a comparable)

At JDA's auctions only. It could be as low as +5.2% or as high as +18.7%.

−2.2%

Extra for a corner among the lowest bids: nothing clear

It could be anywhere from −6.6% to +4.4%, so it may be nothing at all.

At JDA's auctions, a corner's premium shows up in the highest bids, not the lowest. Among the highest bids a corner plot fetched +14.2% more. Among the lowest bids it fetched nothing clear: −2.2%.

What it means for a consultantPrice a corner premium as something the top bids pay, not a fixed add-on. Among the lowest bids, do not count on it.

What it means for the clientA bidder for a corner plot should expect the premium to show in the top bids, not in every sale.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. The two ends were each checked, but the gap between them was not tested on its own.

How I know
  • What I read. Plot auctions at JDA from 2019 to 2026, each compared only with plots in its own scheme.
  • How I compared. The corner premium near the bottom of the range of bids and near the top, after taking out each scheme's own level.
  • How sure. At the top it could be as low as +5.2% or as high as +18.7%. At the bottom, from −6.6% to +4.4%. The two ranges do not overlap.

The tests behind this, for analysts

What a corner adds, among the lowest and the highest bids.

Among the lowest bids a corner adds nothing clear. Among the highest it adds a clear premium.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. 2019 to 2026.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • What about the average? On average a corner adds +6.3% for a house plot and +11.9% for a commercial (shop) plot. The average hides that the premium sits at the top.
  • Could a few schemes drive it? The ranges come from re-drawing whole schemes many times; the top and bottom ranges still do not overlap.
  • Done twice. A second, separate program matched both ends closely.

What this does not show

  • Not the open market. These are JDA's plots in JDA's schemes.
  • Not the gap itself. The difference between top and bottom was not tested on its own; each end was.
  • Not why. The page does not say how many bidders each auction drew.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • What does a corner add at the bottom and the top of the bids? −2.2% at the 10th and +14.2% at the 90th. (Two-step quantile regression after removing scheme effects.)
  • How sure? −6.6% to +4.4%, and +5.2% to +18.7%. (Scheme bootstrap.)
  • On average? House plots +6.3% (+1.0% to +11.8%), commercial plots +11.9% (+6.3% to +17.8%). (The main pricing model, errors clustered by scheme.)
  • Was the arithmetic right? Matched by separately written code. (Independent cross-check.)
