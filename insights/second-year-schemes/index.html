Finding · Timing

A scheme still auctioning in its second year is usually a cheaper scheme.

Use this when you value land near a JDA scheme that is still selling plots a year after it opened.

23%

Higher rates in schemes in their first year of auctions than in schemes still auctioning in their second year, same zone and year

To one decimal place: +23.3%; it could be as low as +10.7% or as high as +37.4%.

A pattern across schemes: cheaper schemes stay on sale longer. Plots do not get cheaper with age (see: No launch discount). Never read it about a named scheme.

At JDA's auctions, schemes in their first year sold about 23% higher than schemes still selling in their second year, in the same zone and year. Inside one scheme there is no such gap. So a scheme that is still selling after a year is usually a cheaper scheme, not a cheaper moment.

What it means for a consultantA scheme still selling after a year is a signal about the scheme. Weigh it when you value land near one.

What it means for the clientA plot buyer looking at a scheme that has been on sale for over a year should ask why, before reading it as a bargain.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. It is a pattern across schemes: the schemes still selling are the cheaper ones. It does not mean a plot loses value as its scheme ages. It is never a statement about a named scheme.

How I know
  • What I read. About 6,600 plot auctions in 458 JDA schemes, each tagged with how old its scheme was at the sale.
  • How I compared. First-year schemes against second-year schemes in the same zone and year, allowing for each plot's size, use and corner. Then the same inside each scheme, scheme against itself.
  • How sure. Across schemes the gap could be as low as +10.7% or as high as +37.4%, and it passed my strictest test. Inside a scheme it was −0.8%: none.

The tests behind this, for analysts

Across schemes, a gap; inside a scheme, none.

The bottom bar compares different schemes in the same zone and year: schemes in their second year sold lower than schemes three or more years old. The top bars compare each scheme with itself: almost no difference at any age.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. Schemes that started from 2007.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it plots ageing? No: inside a scheme, first year against second year, −0.8%.
  • Which number to quote? The first-against-second-year gap, +23.3%. The second-year figure alone, −20.7%, could be chance once every test on the site is counted, so I do not quote it on its own.
  • Done twice. A second, separate program matched it.

What this does not show

  • Not why. The page does not say why a scheme is still selling.
  • Not any one scheme. It is an average across many schemes.
  • Not old schemes. It covers only schemes that started from 2007, because older schemes' start dates are not reliable.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Across schemes, first year against second? +23.3% (95% range +10.7% to +37.4%); passes after correcting for every test on the site. (Cross-section with zone × year effects; Holm correction across 110 tests.)
  • The second-year term alone? −20.7%; does not pass that correction. (Same model.)
  • Inside a scheme? −0.8% (−7.1% to +5.9%). (Scheme fixed effects; errors clustered by scheme.)
