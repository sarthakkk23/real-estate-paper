Lead finding · Land at JDA's auctions

Plots rose far faster than homes, 2019 to 2025.

Use this when you bring an old land value up to today, or a plot owner reads a home-price index as if it were his plot's price.

126%

Rise in plot prices at JDA's auctions, 2019 to 2025

To one decimal place: +125.8%, comparing each scheme only with its own earlier sales.

One government seller's new plots in its own schemes, not Jaipur's whole land market. It could be anywhere from +88.6% to +174.0%, and only 6 schemes sold plots in both years. Zone by zone instead, the rise is +96.7% to +105.0%.

38%

Rise in what banks valued Jaipur homes at, the same years

To one decimal place: +38.1%.

The home includes its building. This index (NHB RESIDEX) averages four quarters, so it moves slowly, and I could read it only once: rounding alone puts it between 37.4% and 38.8%.

From 2019 to 2025, plots at JDA's auctions rose about 126%. Over the same years, the value banks put on Jaipur homes rose about 38%. These are two numbers about two different things. Read them side by side.

What it means for a consultantLand in JDA's schemes and finished homes have moved on different paths since 2019. If you bring a land value up to date with a home-price index, you will not track what JDA-scheme land did.

What it means for the clientA plot owner cannot read a home-price index as the movement of his own plot.

Where it stops One seller's new plots, not Jaipur's whole land market; only 6 schemes sold in both years. The homes figure includes buildings, moves slowly by design, and rests on one reading.

How I know
  • What I read. JDA's published auction results, read 13 November 2026: about 7,300 plot auctions after cleaning (7,316), 4,618 of them from 2019 to 2025. For homes, NHB RESIDEX for Jaipur, each year taken as the average of its four quarters.
  • How I compared. Each JDA scheme is compared only with its own sales in other years, after allowing for the plot's use, size and whether it is a corner. So a cheap new outer scheme cannot drag the figure down.
  • How sure. Re-running the sum many times, each time with a different random pick of schemes, gives between +88.6% and +174.0%. Every reading in the checks below is a large rise.

Nine ways to measure the same rise.

I measured the rise in every sensible way I could. All the ways that compare like with like give a large rise, from +96.7% to +220.4%. I lead with the one that holds the most constant. Only the simple middle price stays low, because it follows where JDA sold, not what land did (how the simple figure hides the rise).

The simple middle price of JDA's winning bids went from ₹29,600 per sq m (₹24,749 per sq yd) in 2019 to ₹43,625 per sq m (₹36,476 per sq yd) in 2025: a rise of +47.4%. It moves with where JDA sold, which is why I do not lead with it.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. "Linked" methods join schemes that sold in neighbouring years, weighted as named.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Could a few unusual schemes explain it? I left out one scheme at a time and re-ran the sum: the rise stayed between +112.9% and +138.0%. Leaving out one zone at a time: +112.5% to +138.2%.
  • Does it depend on 2019 as the start? Starting in 2018, 2020 or 2021 instead gives +143.2%, +128.3% and +127.5%.
  • Do new schemes simply open dearer? I dropped each scheme's first year of sales: +135.7%. Still a large rise.
  • House plots and commercial (shop) plots apart. Residential plots rose +178.8% (it could be as low as +144.3% or as high as +220.6%). Commercial plots rose +94.1%, far less certain: anywhere from +18.9% to +193.7%.
  • Zones JDA did not print. JDA prints a plot's zone only from 4 September 2019, so I filled in the zone for 127 of the 228 auctions of 2019. Without my fill-ins, the zone-by-zone figure reads up to +105.0%; +96.7% is its lowest reading.
  • When it happened. Most of the rise came after mid-2022. Between April-June and October-December 2022 the quarterly figure rose +43.3% and did not fall back. I do not suggest a cause.
  • Done twice. A second, separate program recomputed JDA's headline numbers: 38 of 38 agree.

What this does not show

  • Not Jaipur's whole land market. JDA chooses which plots to sell, when, and at what starting price, mostly in new outer schemes. Its central zones 1 and 3 had 0 plot auctions in 2025-26. Private colonies, society plots and resales are not in it.
  • An indirect link. Only 6 schemes sold plots in both 2019 and 2025 (41 and 31 auctions). The rest of the link runs through 13 to 41 schemes shared by each pair of neighbouring years.
  • Different things. The homes figure is what lenders valued homes at, building included, across the city. NHB builds it with a weighted average of four quarters (Economic Survey 2021-22), so it moves slowly by design. The land figure is winning bids for new plots from one seller. Prices in general (consumer prices) rose +35.8% over the same years.
  • One reading of the homes index. I read it from NHB's website on 13 November 2026; a second try on 15 November failed on errors at NHB's end. Rounding of the 2019 figures alone puts the rise between 37.4% and 38.8%.
  • No forecast. Nothing here says what any one plot is worth, or that the rise will go on.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • How much could the answer move if the sample of schemes had been different? I re-drew whole schemes at random, many times: +88.6% to +174.0%. (Scheme-clustered bootstrap, 95% range.)
  • Is it one scheme or one zone? Each left out in turn: +112.9% to +138.0% by scheme, +112.5% to +138.2% by zone. (Leave-one-out.)
  • What is the model? Each auction's rate per sq m, compared within its own scheme across years, allowing for use, size band and corner. (Hedonic index with scheme fixed effects, log rate per sq m.)
  • Does the method matter? Eight like-for-like methods: +96.7% to +220.4%; the plain median: +47.4%. (Chained matched-scheme indices with four weightings, a stratified median and a zone-level hedonic index.)
  • Is the 2019 base fragile? Base years 2018, 2020, 2021 and dropping first-year sales, as in the checks. (Base-year and entry-effect sensitivity.)
  • Was the arithmetic right? Recomputed from the raw table by separate code: 38 of 38 agree. (Independent cross-check.)
