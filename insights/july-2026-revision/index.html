Finding · Circle rates

The July 2026 circle-rate revision moved each office by a very different amount.

Use this when you redo a stamp-duty cost line written before August 2026, or a buyer asks how much his duty base moved.

+47.7%

Typical rise in residential circle rates in the SANGANER-I office in the July 2026 revision, on the city's edge

19%

The same rise in the walled-city office (JAIPUR-VI); its commercial rates rose only +1.2%

To one decimal place: +18.9%.

The same sample limits; 33 colony rates read in this office.

The July 2026 revision raised circle rates by very different amounts in different offices. In the walled-city office, residential rates rose about 19% and commercial rates barely moved (+1.2%). On the city's edge, residential rates rose +47.7% in SANGANER-I, +47.2% in SANGANER-II and +45.5% in BASSI.

What it means for a consultantA stamp-duty cost line written before August 2026 needs redoing office by office. One city-wide percentage will be wrong in most places.

What it means for the clientA buyer's duty base moved by very different amounts in different parts of the city. Check the office your plot falls under.

Where it stops These are sampled colonies in 7 offices, not weighted by how many sales each sees. 9 of 27 offices could not be dated. A circle rate is the government's minimum value for stamp duty, not a price. No rupee rate is shown on this site.

How I know
  • What I read. A circle rate (also called the DLC rate) is the minimum value the state sets for a property, used to work out stamp duty. Each property falls under a sub-registrar office, the registration office for its area. On e-Panjiyan, the state's registration portal, I read 277 "previous rate" histories for sampled colonies in 7 offices.
  • How I compared. For each colony, the rate after the July 2026 revision against the rate just before it. In each office, the middle step: half the colonies rose more, half less.
  • How sure. None of the 421 colony rates I measured fell (0). It is a sample, so an office's figure could shift if more colonies were read.

The tests behind this, for analysts

Each bar is one office's typical rise for residential rates. The walled city and RAMPURA DABRI barely moved; the edge offices rose by close to half. RAMPURA DABRI rests on only 6 colony rates.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Did any rate fall? None of 421.
  • And commercial rates? JAIPUR-VI +1.2%, JAIPUR-I +18.8%, JAIPUR-II +21.1%, SANGANER-I +47.1%, SANGANER-II +33.0%, BASSI +44.5%, RAMPURA DABRI +1.3%.
  • Was it one revision? Of the 18 offices I could date, 16 show their current rate from 30 July or 31 July 2026.
  • What came before? An April 2026 step raised almost every rate by +10.0%: 410 of 421 colony rates moved by exactly that.

What this does not show

  • Not prices. A circle rate is a floor for stamp duty. What a plot sells for can be far above it.
  • Not every office. Colonies were sampled in 7 offices; 9 offices could not be dated.
  • Not weighted by sales. A colony with few deeds counts the same as a busy one.
  • No rupee rate. The portal's terms do not allow its rates to be reproduced; this site shows steps only.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • How big was each office's step? The middle of the sampled colonies' steps in each office, by use. (Median of colony-level steps.)
  • Did any step fall? 0 of 421. (Complete count of the sample.)
  • When was the rate in force? Dates read from each office's history: 9 offices dated 30 July, 7 dated 31 July. (Portal dates, which may be entry dates.)
