Finding · Timing

There is no reliable month to bid at JDA.

Use this when a client asks whether to wait for a particular month to bid at a JDA auction.

No reliable month

Counted one way, February-March sold +4.2% against the other months (it could be as low as +1.4% or as high as +7.2%), once the steady climb in prices through each year is allowed for

Counted without that allowance, the same months come out −2.0%. The answer flips with the way of counting, and that is the finding.

−2.0%

The same months counted without that allowance

It could be anywhere from −4.7% to +0.9%.

No month at JDA's auctions reliably sells higher. Count one way and February-March looks about 4% dearer; count another and about 2% cheaper. A gap that flips with the counting is not one to plan around.

What it means for a consultantDo not time a JDA bid by the month. Spend the effort on the scheme and the plot.

What it means for the clientWaiting for a "good month" at JDA's auctions gains nothing reliable.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. This is a "no" finding: the sign flips with the way of counting, and that is the point. The number of auctions per month shows no reliable season either.

How I know
  • What I read. 7,316 plot auctions at JDA from November 2003 to 10 November 2026, each compared with its own scheme.
  • How I compared. February-March against the other months, two ways: once allowing for prices climbing steadily through each year, and once without.
  • How sure. Allowing for the climb, February-March looks dearer; without it, slightly cheaper, a gap that could be chance. Neither is a month to plan around.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Why do the two ways differ? Prices tend to rise through a year. February-March sits early in the calendar, so without an allowance for that climb it looks cheap; with it, dear.
  • Is there a busy month? The number of auctions held per month shows no month pattern that passes my test.
  • Is it one era? Every comparison is inside a scheme and a year.

What this does not show

  • Not the open market. Private sales may have their own seasons; for registrations by month, see the tools.
  • Not a rule for one auction. A single auction can do anything.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • February-March with a within-year trend? +4.2% (95% range +1.4% to +7.2%). (The main pricing model with month dummies and a within-year trend for each year.)
  • Without it? −2.0% (−4.7% to +0.9%). (The main pricing model with year effects only.)
  • A season in the number of auctions? p 0.085: no. (Quasi-Poisson monthly counts with year effects; joint test of months.)
