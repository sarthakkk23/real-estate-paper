Lead finding · How sure is a comparable

A comparable from the same scheme and year is good to about 14% either way.

Use this when you value a plot from a comparable sale, or a client is quoted a rate that looks far from the scheme's usual.

±14%

How close a comparable from the same JDA scheme, use and year gets you: 8 in 10 plots sold within about this much of the scheme's usual rate

To one decimal place: ±14.2%.

At JDA's auctions, not the open market.

−28.0% to +38.6%

How far the price actually paid in 2026 sat from my guess, below it to above it, when the rate is carried to another year: 8 in 10 plots fell in this range

One test year only, 2026, and a year of fast rises.

Inside one JDA scheme, one use and one year, 8 in 10 plots sold within about 14% of the scheme's usual rate, either way. Carry that rate to another year and the error widens: when I hid 2026 from my sums and made them guess it, the price actually paid ran from about 28% below my guess to about 39% above it (8 plots in 10).

What it means for a consultantMost of the risk in a valuation is in bringing a rate to another year, not in the plot itself. Carried to a year the sums had not seen, the miss was far wider: from about 28% below the guess to about 39% above it, for 8 plots in 10.

What it means for the clientA buyer quoted a rate far outside the scheme's usual range for that year has a clear question to ask.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions, not the open market. One test year only, and a year of fast rises. The band looked wider in 2020-24, but that could be chance, so I claim no trend.

How I know
  • What I read. 371 groups of plots that share a JDA scheme, a use and a year, each with enough auctions to measure a spread: about 6,000 auctions in all (5,973).
  • How I measured the band. In each group, how far winning rates spread once size and corners are allowed for. I report the typical group.
  • The test on a hidden year. I hid all 338 plot auctions of 2026, fitted my sums on 2003-2025, made them guess 2026, then compared with what really sold.

The tests behind this, for analysts

The band round a comparable opens wide when you change the year.

The dark mark is a comparable's rate. Within the same scheme, use and year, 8 in 10 plots sold inside the short band. Carried to a year the sums had not seen, 8 in 10 landed inside the long one.

How wide the band was in each period.

The band round a same-scheme, same-year comparable, period by period. It looked wider in 2020-24 and narrower again in 2025-26. That rise and fall could be chance; it did not pass my test, so I claim no trend.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. Groups of one scheme, use and year with enough auctions to measure a spread.

How far off a typical guess for 2026 was.

Half of my guesses for 2026's plots were within about 14% of the real price. A simple rule of thumb, the middle price of the same scheme over the last 12 months, was within about 18%. My sums did better, but neither is close enough to price one plot without a fresh comparable.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. The rule of thumb could price 276 of the 338 plots of 2026.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Did the test cheat by seeing 2026? No. 2026 was hidden from the sums entirely; they saw only 2003-2025 and the trend of 2023-25.
  • Was the sums' own warning band honest? Not quite. It should have caught 8 in 10 plots and caught about 7 in 10. So I report the wider band that really happened.
  • How many guesses were close? About 6 in 10 were within 20% of the real price; for the rule of thumb, about 5 in 10.
  • Top bids against low ones. Before allowing for size and corners, the top bids in a typical group sat about 23% above the low ones.

What this does not show

  • Not the open market. The band describes JDA's auctions. A resale or a private colony may spread wider or narrower.
  • Not a trend. The band changed between periods, but not clearly enough to call it a trend.
  • One hidden year. 2026 was a year of fast rises. A calmer year might give a narrower band.
  • Not a valuation tool on its own. It tells you how far to trust a comparable, not what a plot is worth.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • How wide is a comparable? The spread of winning rates inside each scheme, use and year after size and corner, as a band holding 8 in 10: ±14.2%; the 90th bid over the 10th: 1.23 times. (Residual standard deviation of log rate; median cell, 371 cells.)
  • How good is the model on unseen data? Fitted to 2003-2025 with the 2023-25 drift, tested on 338 auctions of 2026: half the errors within 14.1%; errors from −28.0% (10th) to +38.6% (90th). (Out-of-sample holdout; median absolute percentage error.)
  • Was its uncertainty honest? Its 80% band (±25.5%) covered 66.6% of 2026's plots. (Prediction-interval coverage.)
  • Against what baseline? The median of the same scheme and use over the previous 12 months: half the errors within 18.4%. (Naive benchmark.)
  • Was the arithmetic right? Statistics recomputed by separately written code: 272 of 272 agree. (Independent cross-check.)
