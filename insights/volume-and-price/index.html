Finding · Land at JDA's auctions

Fewer auctions in a zone did not mean weaker prices there.

Use this when someone reads fewer JDA auctions in an area as a sign that land there is getting cheaper.

5 of 6 zones

In 2025 JDA sold fewer plots than in 2024 in 6 of the 7 zones I could measure, and prices at its auctions still rose in 5 of them

Plots sold, not plots offered, and one year only. Over all 41 zone-years since 2020 (a zone-year is one zone in one year), a zone's price barely moved with its number of auctions. Nothing here says supply caused anything.

At JDA's auctions, a zone's number of auctions and its price moved independently. In 2025 JDA sold fewer plots than in 2024 in 6 of the 7 zones I could measure, and prices still rose in 5 of them. Over every zone and year since 2020, the link between the two was next to nothing.

What it means for a consultantA fall in JDA auctions in a corridor is not a sign that land prices there are softening. Do not use it as one in a feasibility or absorption note.

What it means for the clientA land owner should not read fewer JDA sales near him as falling values.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. It counts plots sold, not plots offered. It rests on a small set of zones and years. Nothing here says that fewer plots caused anything.

How I know
  • What I read. 41 zone-years at JDA's auctions from 2020 to 2026; a zone-year is one zone in one year.
  • How I compared. For each zone and year: the change in the number of auctions, against the change in price, each scheme compared only with its own earlier sales. Changes common to the whole city in a year were taken out first.
  • How sure. The link I measured is so small that "no link at all" sits well inside its likely range.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it chance either way? I shuffled which zone had which change, many times, inside each year. A link as strong as the one measured turned up by chance often, so there is nothing to explain.
  • Does it depend on my zone labels? JDA prints zones on its page only from 4 September 2019; before that I assigned them. Using only JDA's own printed zones, or dropping 2019, gave the same answer.
  • Was any zone-year left out? 1: zone 7 in 2023, where the one plot that could be compared was also the only corner plot, so its year could not be measured apart from the corner.

What this does not show

  • Not plots offered. The page lists sales. A zone may have offered more plots than it sold.
  • Not cause. Nothing here says fewer plots raised or held prices.
  • Not the open market. These are JDA's own schemes.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Does a zone's price change follow its change in auctions? How far price moved for each one per cent change in auctions: 0.031 per cent (95% range −0.026 to 0.089, so zero sits inside), on 41 zone-years. (Elasticity: log price change on log volume change with year effects; errors clustered by zone.)
  • Could that be chance? Shuffling within year gives p 0.39: nothing beyond chance. (Permutation test within year.)
  • Re-run on JDA's printed zones only, and without 2019: the same answer. (Zone re-check.)
