Finding · Land at JDA's auctions

JDA sold less than half as many plots in 2025 as in 2024, while its prices rose.

Use this when you need to explain the drop in JDA plot sales in 2025, or someone takes it as a sign of falling demand.

55%

Fewer land sales on JDA's results page in 2025 than in 2024: 555 against 1,245

To one decimal place: −55.4%.

Sales, not plots offered; the price agreed, not cash received; JDA's sales, not Jaipur's plot market.

+23.4%

Change in JDA's prices over the same year, each scheme compared only with its own earlier sales

At JDA's auctions only.

JDA's results page lists 555 land sales in 2025 against 1,245 in 2024: about 55% fewer. Over the same year, prices at JDA's auctions rose +23.4%, each scheme compared only with its own earlier sales. Fewer plots came to auction; the ones that did sold for more.

What it means for a consultantRead it with Volume and price: fewer JDA sales in a zone did not go with lower prices there. Do not use it as a sign of a slowing market.

What it means for the clientFewer JDA plots came to auction in 2025. That says nothing about falling prices.

Where it stops It counts sales on JDA's results page, not plots offered. The value is the price agreed at auction, not cash received. These are JDA's sales, not Jaipur's plot market.

How I know
  • What I read. Every land row on JDA's post-auction results page, shop sites included, by year.
  • How I compared. A straight count, year against year.
  • How sure. It is a full count, not a sample. Without the small shop sites the fall is −58.8%, much the same.

The tests behind this, for analysts

Land sales on JDA's results page, by year.

The count rose to 1,245 in 2024 and fell to 555 in 2025. The 2026 bar runs only to 10 November.

JDA's post-auction results page, read 13 November 2026. Every land row, shop sites included.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it the small shop sites? Without them: 1,108 in 2024 and 457 in 2025, a fall of −58.8%.
  • Is 2026 back up? From 1 January to 10 November: 399 in 2026 against 482 over the same dates of 2025, −17.2%.
  • And the value? The price agreed for all land sold: ₹1,496.4 crore in 2024, ₹939.1 crore in 2025.

What this does not show

  • Not plots offered. The page lists sales; it does not list plots that drew no bid.
  • Not cash received. The value is the bid agreed at auction.
  • Not Jaipur's plot market. Private colonies and resales are not on this page.
  • Not why. The page does not say why fewer plots came to auction.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Is the count right? A full count of the page's land rows, recounted by separate code: 38 of 38 JDA headline numbers agree. (Independent cross-check.)
  • Is the price rise like for like? Each scheme compared with its own earlier sales, allowing for use, size and corner: +23.4% from 2024 to 2025. (Same-scheme hedonic index.)
