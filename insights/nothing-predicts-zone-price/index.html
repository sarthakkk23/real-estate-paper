Lead finding · Timing

Nothing in JDA's data tells you next year's price in a zone.

Use this when someone asks "which corridor next?", or a pitch names next year's winning zone from past auction prices. You can stop looking for that answer in JDA's auction data.

No signal worked

Five signals from JDA's auctions, tested on 28 zone-years (one zone in one year): together they guessed next year's price change in a zone worse than the city's average did

A small set, and only the signals JDA publishes. It says these signals do not work, not that nothing ever could.

I tested five signals from JDA's auctions: this year's price change, the number of auctions, the change in that number, how many plots sold at the lowest step, and how many were being re-auctioned. Together, they guessed next year's price change in a zone worse than simply assuming every zone moves with the city's average.

What it means for a consultantThe honest answer to "which corridor next?" from JDA's auction data is that it cannot tell. Any call on a corridor needs other evidence, and you can say so with a test behind you.

What it means for the clientA plot investor should be wary of a pitch that names next year's winning zone from past auction prices.

Where it stops A small set of zones and years. It shows that these signals do not predict; it does not show that nothing could. JDA's lists of plots offered and its starting prices were not available to test.

How I know
  • What I read. For each JDA zone with enough sales, each year from 2020 to 2025: the price change (each scheme against its own earlier sales) and the four other signals.
  • How I tested. I hid one year at a time, let the sums learn from the other years, made them guess the hidden year, and scored the guesses.
  • What I found. Put together, the guesses did worse than simply assuming every zone moves with the city's average.

The tests behind this, for analysts

In 2025, fewer auctions in a zone did not mean lower prices.

One of the signals I tested, shown for one year. From 2024 to 2025, the number of plot auctions fell in almost every zone, yet prices, each scheme against its own earlier sales, rose in most. Knowing the first does not tell you the second.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. Zones with schemes that sold in both years.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding, in the other direction: I tried hard to find a signal that works.

  • Each signal alone. None of the five, on its own, guessed next year's change in a way that passed my test.
  • All together. Worse than the simple city-average guess.
  • Zones JDA did not print. JDA prints zones only from 4 September 2019. I re-ran everything on JDA's own printed zones only, and again leaving 2019 out. The answer did not change.
  • Prices and auction numbers, more broadly. Across zones and years, a zone's price barely moved with its number of auctions. In 2025 JDA sold fewer plots in 6 of the 7 zones I could measure, and prices still rose in 5 of them (see Volume and price).

What this does not show

  • Not that zones never differ. Some zones rose far more than others (zone 9, for one). This says the past did not tell you which, in advance.
  • Not every possible signal. JDA's lists of plots offered and its starting prices were not available. Something outside this data might work.
  • No forecast. Nothing on this page names a zone or a year ahead.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Can five auction signals predict next year's zone price change? Fitted on all years but one and scored on the hidden year, in turn: a score of −0.48 on 28 zone-years, where zero equals the year-average guess. (Leave-one-year-out cross-validated R squared.)
  • Is any single signal related at all? Each tested by shuffling the zone labels within a year and counting how often chance did as well; none passed. (Within-year permutation tests.)
  • Does the result depend on my zone fill-ins? Re-run on JDA's printed zones only and with 2019 dropped: unchanged. (Sensitivity re-check, 15 November 2026.)
  • Do volumes and prices move together? How far price moved for each one per cent change in auctions: 0.031 per cent (95% range −0.026 to 0.089), chance does as well 0.39 of the time, on 41 zone-years. (Elasticity: log price change on log volume change; permutation p.)
