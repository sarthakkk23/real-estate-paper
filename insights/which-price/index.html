Lead finding · Which price, and what a purchase costs

Jaipur's published prices disagree, so name the one you quote.

Use this when a feasibility report or a valuation has to say which price it quotes.

−3.4% to +64.6%

How much Jaipur's published price figures moved, each over its own twelve months, all within July 2022 to December 2024: from builders' quoted prices to new launch prices

Each publisher's twelve months are different, so the periods do not match exactly. No figure is ranked as right: each answers a different question.

Each over its own twelve months, all within July 2022 to December 2024, Jaipur's published price figures ran from −3.4% (builders' quoted prices) to +64.6% (new launch prices). In between: bank valuations of homes +7.6%, registered sale prices −0.3%, plots at JDA's auctions +19.9%.

What it means for a consultantEach figure answers a different question: what a new launch asks, what a bank will lend against, what a deed records, what a plot fetched. Pick the one that matches your question and cite it by its publisher.

What it means for the clientA home buyer can tell the average price of new launches apart from what existing homes are worth.

Two official indices, pointing opposite waysTwo official home-price indices, side by side. The two measure different things, and neither is called wrong here.

How I know
  • What I read. Each figure from its own publisher (PropEquity, NHB, RBI, the listing websites), or worked out here from JDA's results.
  • Checked. I re-read RBI's −0.3% in its release of 31 May 2024. The comparison table was recomputed by separate code and every row agreed.
  • Kept out. A circle-rate index I tried is held back (it rests on too few offices) and is not shown anywhere.

The tests behind this, for analysts

Same city, same years, and the answers do not agree.

Each dot is one published price figure for Jaipur, each over its own twelve months within July 2022 to December 2024. They all start from no change, then move to what each one reported. They do not line up.

PropEquity, NHB RESIDEX, RBI's house price index releases, and JDA's auction results; each read from its publisher.

The most recent year, and the figures still disagree.

The latest twelve months each source covers, all within January 2025 to November 2026. They are closer together than before, but still answer different questions. Housing.com's figure is quoted from a news report, not checked at source. Duty per sale deed is a cost, not a price: it rises when circle rates are revised, even if prices do not. Square Yards does not say over what period its change runs, so it is left out; its city average asking price was ₹4,950 a sq ft in September 2026.

Each publisher's own page or release. Duty per deed includes the March 2026 surcharge rise and the 2026 circle-rate revisions, so it is not a price.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Are the periods the same? Not exactly: each source measures its own twelve months, all within July 2022 to December 2024. The gaps between figures are far larger than a few months could explain.
  • Is any figure misread? Each was read from its publisher; RBI's was read again in its own release. The table was recomputed by separate code, row by row.
  • Do the two official indices really disagree? In 5 quarters, yes: RBI down, NHB up. But RBI prints Jaipur only in quarters when it is its top or bottom city, so those quarters are picked out. In the 2 other quarters both cover, they agree on the direction.
  • Over the long run. From January 2019 to December 2025, NHB's bank valuations rose +38.1% and plots at JDA's auctions +125.8% (plots against homes).

What this does not show

  • Not which is right. Each figure is right for its own question. None is ranked.
  • Not a price for one home or plot. These are city-wide movements.
  • Not the circle rate. Circle rates are minimum values for stamp duty, not prices; a circle-rate index is held back and not shown.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Were the figures read correctly? Every published value re-read from its source; RBI's −0.3% from the release of 31 May 2024. (Source verification.)
  • Was the comparison right? The table of changes recomputed by a separate script, every row agreeing. (Independent cross-check.)
  • Is the RBI-NHB gap selected? RBI publishes Jaipur only as a top or bottom city, so the 5 quarters are a selected sample; 2 unselected quarters agree in sign. (Selection check.)
  • Why is NHB slow? NHB builds RESIDEX with a weighted average of four quarters (Economic Survey 2021-22). (Method note.)
