Finding · Circle rates

Which office you are in decides most of a circle rate.

Use this when you place a site's stamp-duty base, or compare the duty floor of two similar colonies in different parts of the city.

70.6%

Of the gap between two colonies' residential circle rates is explained by which registration office they fall under (the sub-registrar office: the government registration office for the area)

About 7 parts in 10.

It describes the government's rate table, not prices.

69.3%

The same for commercial circle rates

About 7 parts in 10.

The same: a rate table, not prices.

Most of the difference between two colonies' circle rates comes from which office they fall under: about 7 parts in 10, for residential and commercial rates alike. The kind of colony and the road matter, but less.

What it means for a consultantTo place a site's stamp-duty base, find its office first. The colony's type comes second.

What it means for the clientTwo similar colonies in different offices can carry very different duty floors.

Where it stops It describes the government's circle-rate table, not prices. A circle rate is the minimum value for stamp duty. No rupee rate is shown on this site.

How I know
  • What I read. Every base row of the current circle-rate table in the 27 offices that list urban rates. A circle rate (the DLC rate) is the state's minimum value for stamp duty; a sub-registrar office is the registration office for an area.
  • How I compared. I split the spread of all colony rates into two parts: the gap between offices, and the gap between colonies inside the same office.
  • How sure. It is the whole table, not a sample, so there is no sampling error to allow for.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • How far apart are the offices? The typical residential rate in JAIPUR-II is 3.3 times that in JAIPUR-VI; for commercial rates, 3.1 times. Between the dearest and cheapest offices' typical rates: 31 times.
  • Is it the same for commercial rates? Yes: 69.3% for commercial rates against 70.6% for residential.
  • Does the kind of colony add much? Adding it to the office explains somewhat more, but the office carries most of it.

What this does not show

  • Not prices. The table sets a floor for stamp duty; sale prices can differ widely.
  • Not why. The table does not say how each office's rates were set.
  • No rupee rate. The portal's terms do not allow its rates to be reproduced.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • How much of the spread lies between offices? 70.6% residential, 69.3% commercial. (One-way variance decomposition of log base rates by office.)
  • And with the kind of colony as well? A little more. (Two-way decomposition, office × colony kind.)
