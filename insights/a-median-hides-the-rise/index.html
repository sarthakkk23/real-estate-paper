Lead finding · Land at JDA's auctions

The simple middle price hides about half the rise.

Use this when someone quotes the typical auction price, the middle one, as what land did.

47%

Rise in the simple middle price of JDA's winning bids, 2019 to 2025: half sold above it, half below

To one decimal place: +47.4%: ₹29,600 to ₹43,625 per sq m (₹24,749 to ₹36,476 per sq yd).

It follows where JDA sold. Comparing like with like, land rose about 97% zone by zone (+96.7%) and about 126% scheme by scheme (+125.8%).

7 in 10

JDA's plot auctions held in its outer zones 10 to 14 in 2025, up from about 4 in 10 in 2019

To one decimal place: 71.8% in 2025 and 38.2% in 2019.

For 2019, JDA printed zones only from September; on those months alone the share was 43.6%.

The simple middle price of JDA's winning bids rose about 47% from 2019 to 2025. Comparing like with like, land rose about 97% zone by zone and about 126% scheme by scheme. The middle price rose less because, since 2019, JDA has sold far more of its plots in its cheaper outer zones.

What it means for a consultantA middle price of auction results tells you where JDA sold, not what land did. A valuation should use a figure that compares like with like.

What it means for the clientA middle price in a newspaper can understate what comparable land did.

Where it stops Which of the figures that compare like with like is right is a judgement. This shows that a middle price understates JDA's rise, not that any other published figure is wrong.

How I know
  • What I read. The same 4,618 plot auctions of 2019-2025 as the plots against homes finding.
  • Where JDA sold. Zones 10 to 14 had 87 of 228 auctions in 2019 and 326 of 454 in 2025.
  • Zones JDA did not print. JDA prints a plot's zone only from 4 September 2019. On its printed zones alone, 2019's outer share was 44 of 101. Every 2025 zone is JDA's own.

The tests behind this, for analysts

The middle price against figures that compare like with like, 2019 to 2025.

All three lines start level in 2019. The two upper lines compare like with like: they compare each scheme with its own earlier sales, or each zone with itself. The middle price follows where JDA sold, and stays far lower.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026.

Since 2019, far more of JDA's auctions have been in its outer zones.

The share of JDA's plot auctions held in zones 10 to 14, where plots are cheaper. It rose from 2019 and stayed high, though 2025 was below 2024. As the share grew, the middle price was pulled down even while land in each scheme rose.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. For 2019, part of the zones are my fill-ins; on JDA's printed zones alone, 43.6%.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is the low end still double the middle price? The lowest reading that compares like with like is the zone-by-zone figure, +96.7%. Dropping my own zone fill-ins for 2019, it reads from +96.7% to +105.0%. "Hides about half the rise" holds even at that low end.
  • Year by year. From 2019 to 2024 the middle price moved −1.9% while land, like for like, rose +83.0%. From 2024 to 2025 the middle price jumped +50.2% while land rose +23.4%. The middle price swings with where JDA happened to sell that year.
  • A third way. A middle price taken within groups of similar plots in similar places rose +118.4%, close to the figures that compare like with like.

What this does not show

  • Not that other figures are wrong. It shows a middle price understates JDA's rise; it does not judge any other published series.
  • Not the open market. These are JDA's auctions: one government seller's plots in its own schemes.
  • Not which figure that compares like with like is right. That is a judgement; I lead with the one that holds the most constant.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • How much does mix shift move a median? Plain median +47.4% against a stratified median +118.4% and a within-zones hedonic index +96.7%. (Composition effect.)
  • Is the outer-zone shift real or an artefact of my zone fill-ins? On JDA's printed zones alone, 2019's share was 43.6% (44 of 101). (Sensitivity to imputed zones.)
  • Was the arithmetic right? JDA headline numbers recomputed by separate code: 38 of 38 agree. (Independent cross-check.)
