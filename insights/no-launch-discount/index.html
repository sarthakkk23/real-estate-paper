Lead finding · Timing

There is no launch discount inside a JDA scheme.

Use this when a client asks whether to buy at a scheme's launch or wait. You can stop looking for a launch discount at JDA's auctions: inside a scheme, the timing made no difference.

Less than 1%

The difference between plots sold in a scheme's first year and in its second year: they sold for about the same, each scheme compared only with itself

To one decimal place: −0.8%, slightly lower in the first year; it could be anywhere from −7.1% to +5.9%.

At JDA's auctions only. It assumes a scheme's prices stop changing with its age after three years, and it covers schemes that started from 2007.

Inside the same JDA scheme, plots sold in its first year fetched about the same rate as plots sold in its second year: a difference of less than 1%, which is no difference at all. Which scheme you buy in matters; when you buy inside it does not.

What it means for a consultant"Buy at launch" has no support at JDA's auctions. Which scheme matters, not when inside it. You can drop launch timing from the advice.

What it means for the clientA plot investor gains nothing by rushing to a launch, and nothing by waiting, inside the same scheme.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. It assumes a scheme's prices stop changing with its age after three years, and it covers schemes that started from 2007. Other cut-offs were not tried.

How I know
  • What I read. About 6,600 plot auctions in 458 JDA schemes (6,596 auctions), each tagged with how old its scheme was at the sale.
  • How I compared. Each scheme only with itself, after allowing for the zone and year of sale and the plot's size, use and corner.
  • How sure. The difference could be anywhere from −7.1% to +5.9%: either way, too small to plan around.

The tests behind this, for analysts

Same scheme: no gap. Across schemes: a gap, but it is about the scheme.

The top bars compare each scheme with itself, against plots it sold after three years: almost no difference at any age. The bottom bar compares different schemes in the same zone and year: schemes still selling in their second year sold lower. That is a cheaper scheme, not a cheaper moment.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. Schemes that started from 2007.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • First year against second, inside a scheme. −0.8%: no discount, and no premium.
  • Across schemes, the gap is real. Schemes in their first year sold +23.3% above schemes in their second year, in the same zone and year. Inside a scheme that gap disappears, so it is about which schemes are still selling, not about timing.
  • Done twice. A second, separate program fitted the same comparison and matched it.

What this does not show

  • Not the open market. A private developer's launch pricing may differ.
  • Not every cut-off. I assumed a scheme's prices stop changing with its age after three years. Other cut-offs were not tried.
  • Not old schemes. It covers only schemes that started from 2007, because older schemes' start dates are not reliable.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Is there a launch discount inside a scheme? First year against second year, scheme effects plus zone-by-year effects and plot attributes: −0.8% (95% range −7.1% to +5.9%). (Fixed-effects regression, errors clustered by scheme.)
  • What does the same comparison across schemes say? First-year schemes +23.3% above second-year schemes (+10.7% to +37.4%). (Cross-section with zone × year effects.)
  • How is age measured? Months since the scheme's first sale, in yearly bands, held flat after three years. (Age bands with a flat profile after the cap; other caps not run.)
  • Was the arithmetic right? Matched by separately written code. (Independent cross-check.)
