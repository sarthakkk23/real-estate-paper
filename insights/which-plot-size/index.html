Lead finding · Which plot

A big commercial (shop) plot fetches less per sq m; a big house plot barely does.

Use this when you price a large commercial plot, or decide what mix of plot sizes a scheme should sell.

24%

Less per sq m for a commercial (shop) plot of 300-500 sq m (359-598 sq yd) than one of 100-150 sq m (120-179 sq yd), same JDA scheme and year

To one decimal place: −23.8%.

At JDA's auctions only. JDA sets its starting price per sq m, which may pull bids towards one rate.

5%

Less per sq m for a house plot of the same bigger size, same comparison

To one decimal place: −5.4%.

House plots from 25-100 sq m to 300-500 sq m all sat between −5.4% and −0.2% of the base: size barely moves their rate.

At JDA's auctions, a big commercial plot fetches about 24% less per sq m than a mid-sized one in the same scheme and year. A small commercial plot fetches about 14% more. For house plots, size barely changes the rate per sq m.

What it means for a consultantIf you value a large commercial plot in a JDA scheme at one rate per sq m, you overstate it by about a fifth to a quarter. For a residential layout, one rate per sq m across sizes is a fair start.

What it means for the clientA developer bidding for a big commercial plot should expect, and can argue for, a lower rate per sq m. A plot buyer pays about the same rate per sq m for a small or a mid-sized house plot.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. The gap between commercial and house plots could partly be chance: it did not pass my strictest test. JDA sets its starting price per sq m, which may pull bids towards one rate. The very largest house plots rest on few sales.

How I know
  • What I read. About 2,245 commercial-plot auctions and 4,070 house-plot auctions at JDA, each compared only with plots in the same scheme and the same year.
  • How I compared. Each plot's rate per sq m against a plot of 100-150 sq m in the same scheme and year, allowing for corners, the month and whether it was an online auction.
  • How sure. For the big commercial plots, it could be as small as −16.8% or as large as −30.2%. Either way it is a clear discount.

The tests behind this, for analysts

As a commercial plot grows, each sq m is worth less. A house plot hardly changes.

Each square is a plot size, smallest on the left. The fill is what one sq m fetched, against a 100-150 sq m plot in the same scheme and year (the darker square). Commercial plots lose fill as they grow. House plots stay almost level.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. Rates per sq m against a 100-150 sq m plot of the same use, scheme and year.

How much more a commercial plot fetches than a house plot in the same scheme.

The other side of plot choice is use. Since 2020, a commercial plot has fetched about half as much again per sq m as a house plot in the same JDA scheme. Before 2020 the gap was smaller, and it rests on only 4 schemes.

JDA's auction results, read 13 November 2026. Schemes that sold both uses in the same years: 14 in 2020-22, 9 in 2025-26, so the latest figure could be anywhere from +20.0% to +81.6%.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it just a few big schemes? Every comparison is inside one scheme and one year, so a dear scheme cannot pass for a size effect.
  • Every size band of commercial plots, with its range. 150-200 sq m: −9.9%. 200-300 sq m: −18.6%. 300-500 sq m: −23.8%. 500-1,000 sq m: −24.4%. 1,000 sq m and over: −30.1%, somewhere between −40.7% and −17.7%.
  • And for house plots. 150-200 sq m: −3.5%. 200-300 sq m: −0.7%. 500-1,000 sq m: −8.2%. 1,000 sq m and over: −28.3%, but on few sales: anywhere from −46.0% to −4.9%.
  • Comparing within zone and year instead of scheme. For house plots the small size effect disappears: it could be zero. So "size barely matters for house plots" is the safe reading.
  • Done twice. A second, separate program fitted the smallest and the 300-500 sq m commercial bands again and got the same answers.

What this does not show

  • Not the open market. These are JDA's plots in JDA's schemes. A private colony may price size differently.
  • Not why. JDA sets its starting price per sq m. Bidders may start from that rate, which could make sizes look more alike than buyers value them.
  • Not a rule for one plot. It is the typical difference across many sales; a single plot can sit well away from it.
  • The very largest house plots. The 1,000 sq m and over band rests on few sales, so its −28.3% is shown pale and is not a guide.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • What is the model? Each auction's rate per sq m, compared within its own scheme and year, with size bands by use, corner by use, month and auction format. (Log-rate regression with scheme and year fixed effects; errors clustered by scheme.)
  • How fast does the rate fall as a commercial plot grows? Measured as how far the rate per sq m moves for each step up in size, in proportion: −0.145, with a 95% range of −0.209 to −0.081; for house plots −0.047. (Size elasticity, log rate on log area.)
  • Is the gap between commercial and house plots real? −0.098, p 0.0084: clear on its own, but it does not pass once every test on the site is counted together. (Holm correction across 110 tests; 26 pass.)
  • Does the house-plot effect survive a tougher comparison? With zone-and-year effects instead of scheme effects: −0.024, a range that includes zero. (Zone × year fixed effects.)
  • Was the arithmetic right? Statistics recomputed by separately written code: 272 of 272 agree. (Independent cross-check.)
