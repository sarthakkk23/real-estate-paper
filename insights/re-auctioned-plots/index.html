Finding · How sure is a comparable

A re-auctioned plot resells in line with its scheme.

Use this when you value a JDA plot that has come back to auction, or a bidder hopes a returned plot will be a bargain.

+0.7%

The typical gap between a returned plot's change in price and its scheme's change over the same dates

At JDA's auctions only; 109 plots. The results page does not say why a plot came back: never read it as a default or an overbid.

At JDA's auctions, a plot that came back to auction resold in line with its scheme. Its price moved about as much as the scheme's prices did over the same dates: the typical gap was +0.7%, next to nothing. There is no sign that a returned plot sells cheap.

What it means for a consultantValue a returned JDA plot like any other plot in its scheme. A discount for a plot that came back has no support here.

What it means for the clientA bidder on a returned plot should not expect a bargain.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only, on 109 plots. The results page does not say why a plot came back, so never read a return as a default or an overbid.

How I know
  • What I read. 109 plots that JDA sold and that came back to its auction more than three months later.
  • How I compared. Each plot's change in price between its two sales, against how much its scheme's prices moved between the same two dates, each scheme compared only with its own sales.
  • How sure. For each step the scheme moved, a returned plot moved about 1.1 times as much. That figure is uncertain, but the range includes moving exactly in step.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it a few plots? The ranges come from re-drawing whole schemes many times, so one scheme cannot carry it.
  • Are the dates fair? The scheme's movement is measured between the plot's own two sale dates.
  • How many came back? 42 plots in 2025 and 40 in 2026 to 10 November.

What this does not show

  • Not why a plot came back. The page does not say, so this is never about a default, a cancellation or an overbid.
  • Not any single plot. It is the typical pattern across 109 plots.
  • Not the open market. These are JDA's plots in JDA's schemes.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • Does a returned plot move with its scheme? Resale change against the scheme's change: 1.10, where one would mean exactly in step. (Slope of resale log change on the same-scheme index change, index interpolated to the sale dates; scheme bootstrap.)
  • What is the typical gap? +0.7%. (Median of resale change minus index change.)
