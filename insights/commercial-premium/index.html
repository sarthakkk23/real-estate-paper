Finding · Which plot

A commercial (shop) plot fetches about half as much again as a house plot in the same scheme.

Use this when you price commercial plots against house plots inside one planned scheme, or decide how much of a layout to give to commercial plots.

48%

More per sq m for a commercial plot than a house plot in the same JDA scheme, 2025-26

To one decimal place: +47.6%.

Only 9 schemes sold both kinds that year, with just 21 commercial-plot sales among them, so it could be as low as +20.0% or as high as +81.6%. Small commercial plots inside planned schemes, not high-street land.

+57.6%

The same comparison in 2020-22, the period with the most sales

14 schemes. Before 2020 the gap was smaller: +28.7% in 2016-19, on only 4 schemes.

Since 2020, at JDA's auctions, a commercial plot has fetched about half as much again per sq m as a house plot in the same scheme. The gap was +57.6% in 2020-22, +53.9% in 2023-24 and about 48% in 2025-26.

What it means for a consultantInside a planned scheme, a commercial uplift of about half is a steady reference since 2020. Use it to check a pricing note or a product mix.

What it means for the clientA developer pricing commercial plots in his own layout has a public reference from JDA's own sales.

Where it stops At JDA's auctions only. These are small commercial plots inside planned schemes, not high-street land. The latest figure rests on only 9 schemes and 21 commercial-plot sales. "Steady" holds only since 2020.

How I know
  • What I read. Only schemes that sold both commercial and house plots in the same years: in 2020-22, 1,082 auctions in 14 schemes.
  • How I compared. Each commercial plot against house plots in the same scheme, allowing for the year, the plot's size and whether it was a corner.
  • How sure. Over all years together, for a plot of about 150 sq m, the gap is +51.3%. It could be as low as +36.5% or as high as +67.8%, and it passed my strictest test.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it a dear scheme passing for a commercial premium? No: every comparison is inside one scheme.
  • Is it a different day passing for a premium? Comparing only plots sold in the same scheme on the same auction day, 181 auctions in 2020-22, the gap was +58.0%.
  • How wide is each period's figure? 2020-22: +47.9% to +68.1%. 2023-24: +36.5% to +73.4%. 2025-26: +20.0% to +81.6%.

What this does not show

  • Not high-street land. These are commercial plots JDA sells inside its own planned schemes.
  • Not every year. Before 2020 few schemes sold both kinds of plot, so the earlier gap is a weak guide.
  • Not why. The page records the winning bid, not what the bidder planned to build.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • How big is the gap in each period? Rate per sq m compared within scheme, with year, size and corner allowed for: +57.6% in 2020-22 (95% range +47.9% to +68.1%). (Log-rate regression with scheme fixed effects, by period; errors clustered by scheme.)
  • Does it hold over all years at one size? +51.3% for a plot of about 150 sq m, and it passes once every test on the site is counted together. (The main pricing model; Holm correction across 110 tests, 26 pass.)
  • Same scheme, same day? +58.0% on 181 auctions. (Scheme × auction-day fixed effects.)
