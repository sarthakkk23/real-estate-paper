Finding · Circle rates

The walled city's circle rates were back at their 2014 level by December 2024.

Use this when you read an old-city circle rate as a guide to how values there have changed, or compare duty floors across the central offices.

About where it was in 2014

The walled-city office's (JAIPUR-VI) typical residential circle rate on 1 December 2024 against October 2014: a change of −0.8%

Only 3 offices, before inflation. It describes the rate table, not prices. No rupee rate is shown.

+38.7%

The same change in JAIPUR-I; in JAIPUR-II it was +36.6%

The same limits: sampled colony rates, before inflation.

On 1 December 2024, the walled-city office's typical circle rate was where it stood in October 2014: −0.8% for residential rates and −1.6% for commercial. Over the same years, residential rates in JAIPUR-I rose +38.7% and in JAIPUR-II +36.6%.

What it means for a consultantIn the old city, two cuts (in 2019 and on 24 February 2021) and three rises nearly cancelled out. The circle rate there is not a guide to how values changed.

What it means for the clientAn old-city owner's duty base had barely moved between 2014 and the 2026 revisions.

Where it stops Only 3 offices have rate histories back to 2014 for enough colonies. The changes are before inflation; figures after inflation are held back for now. It describes the government's rate table, not prices. No rupee rate is shown.

How I know
  • What I read. A circle rate (the DLC rate) is the state's minimum value for stamp duty, set for each colony under a sub-registrar office. On the state's portal I read each sampled colony's dated rate history: 28 residential and 30 commercial colony rates in JAIPUR-VI.
  • How I compared. Each colony's rate on 1 December 2024 against its rate in October 2014; then the middle change in each office: half the colonies moved more, half less.
  • How sure. These are dated records, not estimates. The sample is small: 31 colony rates in JAIPUR-I and 38 in JAIPUR-II.

The tests behind this, for analysts

Change in circle rates, October 2014 to December 2024, by office.

JAIPUR-VI is the walled-city office. Its rates ended about where they began. The two central offices beside it rose, residential rates most.

The state's circle-rate portal (e-Panjiyan), rate histories read 13 November to 15 November 2026. Changes only; no rupee rate. Before inflation.

How I checked it

I tried to break this finding. Each line asks one question a doubter would ask.

  • Is it one colony? It is the middle of 28 residential colony rates in the walled-city office.
  • What happened since? The 2026 revisions raised the walled-city office's rates again; see The July 2026 revision.
  • Commercial too? Yes: −1.6% in JAIPUR-VI, against +13.0% in JAIPUR-I and +13.0% in JAIPUR-II.

What this does not show

  • Not prices. A circle rate is a floor for stamp duty; sale prices can move quite differently.
  • Not why. The record shows the dates and sizes of each change, not the reasons.
  • Not after inflation. Those figures are held back for now.
  • Not other offices. Only 3 offices have histories this long.

The tests behind this (for analysts)

For analysts: what each test asked, and what it found. The technical name is in brackets.

  • What was each office's change? The middle of colony-level changes, by use. (Median of dated colony histories, nominal.)
  • Is a sampling test meaningful? The colonies are a sample, so each office's figure could shift with more colonies; no interval is computed. (Descriptive, sampled.)
